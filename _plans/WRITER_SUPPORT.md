# Writer support, stage 2

**Status: In progress.** Tracking issue: #1291. The shipped writer is described in [WRITER.md](../_designs/WRITER.md), [WRITER_INPUT.md](../_designs/WRITER_INPUT.md), [WRITER_ENCODING.md](../_designs/WRITER_ENCODING.md) and [WRITER_VALIDATION.md](../_designs/WRITER_VALIDATION.md).

Stages 1–20a, 21a, 26a, 27, 28, 31, 36 and 37 shipped under #9 and #1083, stage 22 under #1454, stage 23a under #1455, chunk-sized dictionary probes under #1521, stage 24 under #1426, stage 25 under #1449, stage 30 under #985, stage 38 under #1499 and stage 39 under #1513; stage 26b was superseded when the dictionary size cap was removed. Stage 32 (`ColumnBatch.strings(…)` and a packed binary input form, #1424) was dropped: copying a binary column from `ColumnReader` to `ColumnBatch` took the same time through `getBinaries()` and `byte[][]` as through the reader's packed buffer (4M rows of two `STRING` columns: 283 ms against 285–296 ms uncompressed, 680 ms against 674–688 ms under ZSTD), the writer's encoding being the cost. Stage numbers are stable identifiers from that plan, not positions, so the open ones below keep their numbers.

| 33 | **Group-oriented nesting for columnar copying** (see `ColumnarNestedCopyTest`): a nested file can be copied from `ColumnReader` to `ColumnBatch` byte-identically today, but only through four corrections a reasonable first attempt gets wrong, three of them silently rather than loudly. Three share one root cause: the reader describes nesting per *leaf*, as layers beneath it (`getLayerCount`, `getLayerKind(k)`, `getLayerValidity(k)`, `getLayerOffsets(k)`), and the writer addresses it per *group*, by dot path (`struct`, `list`, `map`). Nothing bridges the two, so a caller reimplements the reader's own layer-derivation rule — which groups contribute a layer, and that the synthetic `list` / `key_value` segment is a path segment but not a layer — and then de-duplicates the shared ancestors a per-leaf walk reports more than once, against an "already set" rejection. A subtle error in that derivation misaligns validity and offsets against groups rather than failing. Naming the layers — `ColumnReader.getLayerPath(int)`, plus a `LayerKind` that separates `MAP` from `LIST` since the writer's verbs reject each other's groups — removes the derivation but leaves the shape mismatch and the de-duplication standing. Projecting the same buffers group-wise instead, as one accessor on `ColumnReaders` yielding each nesting group once with its path, kind, validity and offsets, removes all three and reduces the copy to "set every group, then set every leaf". The per-leaf layer view stays as it is: it is the right shape for a consumer reading one column, and the wrong one for a caller feeding a whole file to a writer. The fourth correction is the writer's and is independent of the other three: `getLeafValidity()` answers "is there a value in this slot" and is false wherever an `OPTIONAL` ancestor is absent, whereas the writer's leaf mask answers "is this column's own value null" and is refused outright on a `REQUIRED` column — so a `REQUIRED` leaf under an `OPTIONAL` struct reports nulls the writer will not accept, and for `BYTE_ARRAY` those same slots arrive as Java `null`s the all-present setter rejects and the caller must plug with a placeholder first. Both halves are the same gap: the writer validates a leaf against its own declared repetition without consulting the ancestor masks that decide whether a slot is encoded at all. `ColumnBatch` already holds the principle — "the values at rows a column's `Validity` marks null are never encoded, and so are never checked" — and extending it to ancestor-absent rows costs the property that a rejection names the setter call it came from, because ancestors may be declared after leaves: either leaf validation defers to submit, or groups must precede leaves in a batch, and that ordering rule is its own hazard. The reader-side alternative is to expose the leaf's own null bit separately from the present-in-slot mask, which is a bitmap the reader does not compute today. Acceptance is concrete rather than descriptive: the copier in `ColumnarNestedCopyTest` loses its derivation, its de-duplication set, its repetition branch and its hole-plugging, and still produces a byte-identical file. | Layer | Columnar copy is a pass-through, not a reimplementation of the reader's layer rule | 6.3 | [ ] |

| 34 | **`StructBuilder.setValue(String\|int, Object)`**: the runtime-typed escape hatch mirroring the reader's `getValue`, and the last unmatched entry between `StructAccessor` and `StructBuilder` that is closable. The reserved-surface rule in [WRITER_INPUT.md](../_designs/WRITER_INPUT.md#reserved-surface) forbids only an unqualified `Object`-typed `set` / `add` competing with typed siblings, so a distinctly named `setValue` is admissible. What does hold is the original rationale for its absence: a reader must handle a column whose type it learns at runtime, whereas "a writer always knows the schema it declared". That is true of application code and false of the case that motivates this stage — a copier, a projection, or a format bridge, which knows the schema only at runtime and today must switch on `PhysicalType` by hand to pick a setter. So this stage is a Variant *writer*, of which `setVariant` is the surface: the encoder, its own design document, the choice of whether to write the unshredded `metadata`/`value` pair only or to shred into `typed_value` as the read path already reassembles, and interop-gate coverage against parquet-java's and Arrow's Variant readers. Sized and sequenced accordingly — it is a breadth stage in its own right, not a setter to be added alongside stage 34. | Breadth | `VARIANT` columns are writable, not only readable | 6.4 | [ ] |

## Ordering

23b is sized against what 23a measured: on a 6-column taxi-shaped fixture of 4M rows, parallel encoding took a write from 417 ms to 274 ms under ZSTD and from 230 ms to 181 ms uncompressed, the narrow schema bounded by its most expensive chunk; on a 100-column fixture from 3362 ms to 1721 ms under ZSTD and from 1849 ms to 1465 ms uncompressed (8 vCPUs). What remains on the caller's thread is accumulation and the drain; 42 takes the accumulation, about 150 ms of the taxi fixture's 273 ms ZSTD write after #1521, and is argued on a measurement of how that splits across columns. 43 lifts the cap a narrow compressed schema hits at its most expensive chunk, taxi's `pickup_ts` at 42% of the flush. 23b, 42 and 43 are independent of each other. 40 is independent of the others.

33 is not sequenced ahead of them. Its evidence of demand is `ColumnarNestedCopyTest` alone, and its design has an open fork: leaf validation deferred to submit, or groups ordered before leaves in a batch. The common reason to copy a file is a rewrite or a merge, which #791 and #767 serve by byte-copying row groups instead of decoding and re-encoding them, so 33 is weighed against those when it is picked up.

29 is parked pending apache/parquet-format#628, which may redefine `distinct_count` as an estimate; if it does, the gaps can be filled from a cardinality sketch instead of an exact count.

34 and 35 are the two reader accessors with no setter, and are independent of each other and of the rest: 34 is a decision about the `Object`-typed setter rule, and 35 is a Variant encoder that happens to surface as a setter.

Changing the `WriterConfig` at a caller-placed boundary (`endRowGroup(WriterConfig)`, so a file mixes codecs or encodings across row groups) was dropped from stage 30 for want of a use case: neither Arrow C++, arrow-rs nor parquet-java offers it, and `AUTO` already re-decides each chunk's encoding per row group. It is picked up when a caller names a need two files cannot serve.

Sequenced as separate later milestones, each its own design and sequence: DataPage V2, the Avro write adapter, and a CLI write/convert command.

## Parallel write model

A write moves through three steps, each overlapping the next by a bounded amount:

| Step | Runs on | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Accumulate | the caller's thread | one row group's retention |
| Encode and compress | the pool of a `HardwoodContext` and the caller's thread, one column chunk at a time (23a) | encoded chunks held until the drain reaches them |
| Drain to the `OutputFile` | sequentially, in schema order | encoded chunks waiting for an earlier one, plus, under 23b, a queue of encoded bytes |

The sink may overlap further on its own: `S3OutputFile` uploads up to four parts while the writer fills the next (stage 22), so S3 latency is hidden without the writer knowing. Pipelining whole row groups, with the next accumulating while the previous encodes, would multiply retention by the number in flight and is not planned; 23b gives the overlap that matters for output at the cost of encoded bytes only.

What each step must keep: byte-identical output to a single-threaded write; a failure in any task fails the write and discards the output, never swallowed and never hanging the caller; a `ParquetFileWriter` confined to one caller thread at a time. 23a's shape is in [WRITER.md](../_designs/WRITER.md#threading).
